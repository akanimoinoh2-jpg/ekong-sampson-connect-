01 / About Ekong Sampson

A career in public service.

His career spans local government, state legislative and executive service, and representation in the National Assembly.

Senator Ekong Sampson, Ph.D, is a journalist, author, lawyer, and public servant from Mkpat Enin, Akwa Ibom State. He has served as Councilor and Chairman of Mkpat Enin LGA (2003-2007), State House of Assembly member (2007-2015), and Akwa Ibom State Commissioner (2015-2020). Elected to the Nigerian Senate in 2023, he chairs the Senate Committee on Solid Minerals Development and is Vice Chairman of the Committee on Basic and Secondary Education. He is known for his advocacy in education, human capital development, and infrastructural projects.

Early Career and Political Development

From 1999 to 2003, Sampson served as a legislative aide to Senator Udoma Udo Udoma, gaining experience with the National Assembly and the legislative process. He later served as Chairman of Mkpat Enin Local Government Area and represented Mkpat Enin State Constituency in the Akwa Ibom State House of Assembly for two terms.

He also served as Special Adviser on Political and Legislative Affairs to the Akwa Ibom State Government. His subsequent executive appointments covered Rural Development, Economic Development, Labour and Manpower Planning, and Environment and Petroleum Resources. These roles involved political coordination and work across rural development, economic planning, labour and environmental and petroleum-related affairs.

A fuller assessment of his work in these offices would require the relevant government records, policy documents and project information.

Earlier Legislative Career in Akwa Ibom State

Sampson’s two terms representing Mkpat Enin State Constituency in the Akwa Ibom State House of Assembly preceded his election to the federal Senate and gave him experience in state-level lawmaking and representation. A comprehensive account of this period would document bills sponsored or co-sponsored, motions, committee assignments, oversight activities and laws passed during his tenure.

Any account should distinguish projects he personally facilitated from those undertaken by the Akwa Ibom State Government or its agencies. His subsequent executive appointments in Rural Development, Economic Development, Labour and Manpower Planning, and Environment and Petroleum Resources warrant separate examination through government records, policy documents and ministry reports. Research on his Environment and Petroleum Resources portfolio should be based on documented policies, interventions and official actions, particularly in relation to environmental management and petroleum-producing communities.

Election to the Nigerian Senate

Sampson was elected to represent Akwa Ibom South Senatorial District in the February 2023 general election and became a member of the 10th National Assembly. His election marked his transition from state-level politics to federal legislative representation.

The district’s interests include education, healthcare, roads, electricity, agriculture, maritime activities, environmental protection and economic opportunities linked to the area’s natural resources. As a senator, his responsibilities include representing constituents, participating in federal lawmaking and legislative debate, overseeing government institutions, and advocating policies that address the district’s needs.

Legislative Responsibilities and Committee Work

Public reporting has identified Sampson as Chairman of the Senate Committee on Solid Minerals Development and Vice Chairman of the Committee on Education (Basic and Secondary). These committee assignments provide an institutional role in engaging with mineral-sector development, education policy and oversight of relevant government activities. He has also been identified as a member of the Senate Committee on Marine Transport.

Legislative work relating to solid minerals can include examining mining regulation, investment, environmental concerns, revenue generation and the formalisation of mining activities—an important part of Nigeria’s efforts to diversify its economy beyond petroleum. Committee responsibilities in basic and secondary education provide opportunities to examine education policy, federal interventions, institutional funding and challenges affecting access to quality education.

The significance of these roles is best evaluated through specific committee reports, hearings, oversight visits, recommendations and legislative outcomes rather than committee titles alone.

Political Development and Party Affiliation

Public reporting recorded Sampson’s movement from the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) to the All Progressives Congress (APC) in July 2025. This development forms part of the political history of his Senate tenure and the wider political landscape in Akwa Ibom State.

Conclusion

Senator Akpan Ekong Sampson’s public career extends from legislative assistance and local government administration to state-level lawmaking, executive appointments and representation in the Nigerian Senate.

His experience across these institutions provides a basis for examining his approach to legislation, public administration and constituency development. His reported Senate activities and constituency interventions also raise questions about the relationship between legislative representation, project delivery and measurable public benefit.

A complete and authoritative profile will require a detailed timeline of his bills, motions, committee reports, previous Assembly activities and constituency projects, supported by official records and independent verification.
